Scope note. Sections tagged exact are computed over the complete filtered result set. For most breakdowns that's OpenAlex's server-side group_by aggregation. For Impact, it's by paging through every matching work individually, since averaging FWCI and citation percentiles isn't something group_by can do — capped at 10,000 works as a safety limit; a note under the Impact KPIs will say so if you hit it. For the Collaboration country/type breakdowns, it's the exact list of partner institutions (from group_by, with USN's own entry excluded) cross-referenced against each partner's real country/type record — capped at the 150 largest partners, noted if truncated. Only International % under the top KPI row remains a random-sample estimate, since it answers a different, per-work question ("does this specific work have a non-Norwegian co-author") that the aggregate breakdowns don't capture. One asymmetry worth knowing: clicking "Norway" or "Education" will filter the whole page to works with any Norwegian or education-type co-author — which includes USN-only works too, since USN itself is Norwegian and education-type. The displayed count next to those two rows excludes USN's own contribution, but the resulting filter can't.

      
Cross-filtering. Clicking any row anywhere on the page adds it as an active filter (shown as a chip below the top KPIs) and refreshes every panel — Volume, Impact, and Collaboration alike — to match. Selecting a new value for a field that already has an active filter replaces it rather than stacking; filters across different fields combine with AND. Clear individual filters from their chip, or everything at once from the bar. In live mode this re-queries OpenAlex directly; with "Restrict to NVA ∩ OpenAlex" checked, it instead re-filters the same already-matched work list client-side, since that list comes from batched DOI lookups rather than a live filterable query — either way the result is the same instant re-filtering.

      
NVA category. Only populated with "Restrict to NVA ∩ OpenAlex" checked — it's NVA's own classification of each matched publication, not something OpenAlex has any concept of, so a live unrestricted query has nothing to show here. It's a separate taxonomy from OpenAlex's own "Work types" panel next to it; the two won't always agree on how a given work should be categorized, and that disagreement is itself real information, not a bug to reconcile.

      
Institution matching. USN's own entry is identified as whichever result has a joint-work count equal to the query's total — a small logical trick, but a reliable one: since every matching work is required to include USN by construction of the search, USN's own count will always equal the total exactly, regardless of what ID format a given breakdown happens to use.

      
On FWCI. OpenAlex's field-weighted citation impact is a real, documented metric, but independent attempts to reproduce it have found meaningful discrepancies in some cases. Treat it as a strong indicator, not an audited figure — consistent with how CoARA/DORA recommend using any single citation metric.

      
Institution matching. OpenAlex parses raw affiliation strings algorithmically; figures may differ slightly from NVA's own registry, particularly for non-English or non-DOI'd outputs.

Author & Research Group Profiles

        
Pull a publication list with impact, field, and collaboration context for one researcher or a whole group. Uses the year range, work types, open access, journal, publisher, and author-count filters above — the institution field above is not used here, since you select people directly below instead. In line with the Leiden Manifesto and DORA, this deliberately avoids reducing anyone to one number: impact is shown as a distribution across a portfolio, not an average, and h-index-style single indicators are tucked behind an explicit toggle rather than presented as headline figures. OpenAlex computes its own field-weighted citation impact (FWCI) directly from its citation graph — labeled "FWCI" throughout this tab — which is not the same figure as Scopus SciVal's FWCI for the same paper, since the two draw on different citation databases. Each publication with a DOI also carries a small Dimensions citation badge, loaded live from Dimensions' own servers — this is a third-party embed, not data this app fetches and stores itself, and requires USN to be registered for Dimensions Badge/Metrics API use for compliant display.

Subfield Co-occurrence Map

      
Uses the institution, year range, and every filter set above. Fetches each matching work's OpenAlex Subfields (up to 3 per work, deduplicated to one entry per subfield per work), then maps which subfields get combined within the same USN work — a way to explore interdisciplinary connections a single-dimension table can't show. The layout and clustering are computed here in the browser: a small hand-rolled force simulation and a single-pass greedy modularity clustering, not VOSviewer's own algorithm. Visually similar in spirit to a VOSviewer term map; a genuinely different method underneath.

Publication Search

      
Relevance-ranked search against titles and abstracts, restricted to USN and the year range and filters set above — this is OpenAlex's own relevance ranking (word- and phrase-matching with some ranking sophistication), not meaning-based semantic search. A query about "financial hardship" won't reliably surface a paper about "economic precarity" unless the words themselves overlap enough — that's the real limitation of this approach, not a bug. Results are ordered by relevance; the underlying relevance score itself isn't a meaningful number on its own (no fixed scale, not a percentage), so it isn't shown — only the ordering it produces.

Level 1 / Level 2 channel share exact

      

        
Restricted to publications in a channel (journal, series, or publisher) that's actually rated Level 1 or 2 in the national register — the great majority of NVA's ~50 categories (lectures, media pieces, degree theses, and more) never carry a channel rating at all, and aren't counted as "Level 0" here; they're simply outside what this indicator measures.

        

      

    


    

      
Publication points exact*

      

        
*Computed against the official DBH weight table and the post-2015 formula (weight × √(institution share) × 1.3 if international) — see the code comments in the snapshot builder for the exact methodology and its sources. The Norwegian National Publishing Committee's own guidance explicitly warns against using this indicator at small-unit or individual level, since it was designed and validated for institution-wide funding allocation, not for assessing a department or a person. At faculty level this is a reasonable, standard use; the smaller the unit you're looking at below, the more that warning applies — treat department-level point totals as a rough signal, not a verdict.

        

        

        

      

    


    

      
Open access exact*

      

        
*Inferred from whether NVA has a publicly downloadable file attached to the record — this tracks accessibility (can someone read it for free), not reuse licensing (can they adapt or redistribute it); a record counted as "open" here may still carry a restrictive copyright license. The overall rate blends every NVA category together, including many (lectures, media interviews, conference posters) that would rarely have an uploaded file regardless of openness policy — the by-category breakdown below is the more meaningful comparison.

        

      

    


    

      
Co-publishing exact

"Unspecified institution" is NVA's own placeholder for a contributor affiliation that couldn't be resolved to a real institution, not a genuine collaborator.

Coverage Check

      
Institution-wide only for the core check — this can't be scoped to a faculty or department the way other NVA-integrated tabs are. The reason is structural, not a missing feature: restricting the "is this in NVA at all" reference set to one faculty would make a work tagged to a different faculty look like it's missing from NVA entirely, when it's really just filed elsewhere — the same problem applies to the NVA category filter for the same reason. What this tab does instead: the core matched/not-matched/no-DOI split always checks against NVA's complete DOI universe, and the "Matched works by faculty" section below gives the faculty-level view for the matched side specifically, without weakening that core check. Uses the shared year range, work type, open access, author count, journal, and publisher filters above, with USN's institution fixed regardless of what's entered in the shared institution field elsewhere in this app. A work landing in "Not matched to NVA" doesn't mean NVA is wrong — it could be a genuine registration gap, but could equally be a work OpenAlex has mis-attributed to USN via affiliation-string matching, a preprint, a non-USN-authored item, or simply a different DOI recorded on the NVA side. Treat the list below as a starting point for manual review, not a verdict.

Funder Analysis

      
Uses OpenAlex's funders data — a genuinely new part of their schema (their older grants field on works has been removed entirely, replaced by this and a separate Awards entity). Respects Explorer's "Restrict to NVA ∩ OpenAlex" checkbox above: off shows USN's whole live OpenAlex portfolio using the shared institution, year, type, OA, journal, and publisher filters; on scopes to whichever faculty/department/NVA category selection is checked, matched by DOI, the same as Explorer and Segment Comparison.

      
A funder linked to a work is not necessarily a funder of USN's own contribution to that work — OpenAlex attaches funder acknowledgements to the whole paper, not to specific co-authors. A USN researcher who is one of many co-authors on an internationally collaborative paper can show that paper's other funders (a Chinese, Japanese, German, or other foreign funding body, say) attached to USN's own portfolio, with no real connection between that funder and USN. This is why the breakdown below splits Norway-based funders (and USN itself) — where genuine attribution is far more plausible — from every other country, shown separately and clearly labeled, never silently merged into one ranked list.

      
Individual grant/award-level data (specific grant numbers, principal investigators) isn't used here. OpenAlex's new Awards entity currently has empty investigator fields across the board and often aggregates by national funding program name (e.g. "973 Program," "Sigma2") rather than individual grant, funding tens of thousands of unrelated works at once — not precise enough to build on yet. Worth revisiting if that data matures.
