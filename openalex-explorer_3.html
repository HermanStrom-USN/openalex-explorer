Scope note. Sections tagged exact are computed over the complete filtered result set. For most breakdowns that's OpenAlex's server-side group_by aggregation. For Impact, it's by paging through every matching work individually, since averaging FWCI and citation percentiles isn't something group_by can do — capped at 10,000 works as a safety limit; a note under the Impact KPIs will say so if you hit it. For the Collaboration country/type breakdowns, it's the exact list of partner institutions (from group_by, with USN's own entry excluded) cross-referenced against each partner's real country/type record — capped at the 150 largest partners, noted if truncated. Only International % under the top KPI row remains a random-sample estimate, since it answers a different, per-work question ("does this specific work have a non-Norwegian co-author") that the aggregate breakdowns don't capture. One asymmetry worth knowing: clicking "Norway" or "Education" will filter the whole page to works with any Norwegian or education-type co-author — which includes USN-only works too, since USN itself is Norwegian and education-type. The displayed count next to those two rows excludes USN's own contribution, but the resulting filter can't.

      
Cross-filtering. Clicking any row anywhere on the page adds it as an active filter (shown as a chip below the top KPIs) and refreshes every panel — Volume, Impact, and Collaboration alike — to match. Selecting a new value for a field that already has an active filter replaces it rather than stacking; filters across different fields combine with AND. Clear individual filters from their chip, or everything at once from the bar.

      
Institution matching. USN's own entry is identified as whichever result has a joint-work count equal to the query's total — a small logical trick, but a reliable one: since every matching work is required to include USN by construction of the search, USN's own count will always equal the total exactly, regardless of what ID format a given breakdown happens to use.

      
On FWCI. OpenAlex's field-weighted citation impact is a real, documented metric, but independent attempts to reproduce it have found meaningful discrepancies in some cases. Treat it as a strong indicator, not an audited figure — consistent with how CoARA/DORA recommend using any single citation metric.

      
Institution matching. OpenAlex parses raw affiliation strings algorithmically; figures may differ slightly from NVA's own registry, particularly for non-English or non-DOI'd outputs.

Institution Comparison

        
Uses the year range set above. Compares USN against other institutions on rate-based metrics (so a small institution isn't penalized just for publishing less) alongside total volume for scale. No fixed cap on how many — compare all 10 Norwegian universities if you want. Worth knowing: every institution does a full paginated fetch, and all fetches share the same global rate limit, so comparing all 10 from a cold cache can genuinely take a couple of minutes, not seconds. Each institution's result is cached separately for 30 days, though, so every run after the first is instant unless you force-refresh.

Up-and-coming researchers

          

            
Same split-period logic, counting only USN-affiliated works. Deliberately volume-based, not impact-based — an individual's yearly output is too small a sample for a recency-adjusted FWCI trend to mean much.

            
New — no works at all in the earlier half; at least 2 needed in the recent half to count

            

            
Growing — active in both halves, with at least 3 works total

            

          

        

      


      
This is trend detection over your selected period, not forecasting — it describes a trajectory that already happened, not a prediction of what comes next. A two-bucket split is a deliberately simple, auditable method rather than a statistical model; it can be thrown off by one unusual year, especially for researchers with only a handful of works. Treat "up and coming" as a prompt to look closer, not a verdict.

External Collaboration

        
Uses the institution and year range set above. Looks specifically at co-authorship with non-academic institutions — company, government, healthcare, nonprofit, facility, and archive types, as distinct from education. This is a societal-engagement signal, not a quality or impact ranking: NOR-CAM and CoARA treat collaboration breadth as its own merit dimension, evaluated on its own terms, not as a multiplier on scientific performance. It also only captures what shows up as co-authorship in OpenAlex — advisory work, policy input, and other forms of societal interaction that never produce a joint publication won't appear here. One full fetch of the matching works, classified client-side, same caching as the other tabs.

Author & Research Group Profiles

        
Pull a publication list with impact, field, and collaboration context for one researcher or a whole group. Uses the year range, work types, open access, journal, publisher, and author-count filters above — the institution field above is not used here, since you select people directly below instead. In line with the Leiden Manifesto and DORA, this deliberately avoids reducing anyone to one number: impact is shown as a distribution across a portfolio, not an average, and h-index-style single indicators are tucked behind an explicit toggle rather than presented as headline figures. OpenAlex computes its own field-weighted citation impact (FWCI) directly from its citation graph — labeled "FWCI" throughout this tab — which is not the same figure as Scopus SciVal's FWCI for the same paper, since the two draw on different citation databases.
